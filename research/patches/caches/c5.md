## What it does

`ObtainBuffer`, `ObtainBufferForImage` and the upload paths call `TextureCache::ResolveReadbacks` 3-4 times a
draw. With readbacks always queued (UE4's HZB and exposure), each call took `readback_mutex` and scanned the queue.
Now `PublishReadbackSpans` publishes the pending copies' pages as at most 4 merged ranges (`ReadbackQueue::Spans`)
through a seqlock (`readback_spans`). Calls with source `BufferObtain`, `BufferForImage` or `BufferUpload` return
"nothing to wait for" without the lock when `ReadbackSpansMiss` finds their range on none of them.

## Invariants

- The spans must be a superset of what `RequiredTick` can match: page-rounded like `OverlapsPage`, an empty copy
  still covers one byte, merging only adds pages, and more than 64 copies publish "everything". Making them
  tighter than `RequiredTick` skips a needed wait: the GPU reads guest memory before the readback has landed.
- `PublishReadbackSpans` runs under `readback_mutex` after every `Push` (the only change that adds pages) and
  before `has_pending_readbacks` is set. `Retire` / `Cancel` republish only to tighten. A new path that adds pending
  pages must publish too.
- Seqlock: the writer makes `seq` odd, a release fence, relaxed stores, then `seq` even with release. The reader
  re-checks `seq` after an acquire fence. An odd or changed `seq` falls back to the lock, never past it.
- Only the buffer cache's sources skip the lock. They run on the thread that queues the copies, or after a
  draw-pipe drain. The CPU fault paths (`CpuRead` / `CpuWrite`) must keep the lock: a page is watched before its
  copy is pushed, so a fault can arrive before the spans include it.
- Toggle word 2 bit 128 (live): the lock and the scan every time.

## Interactions

- `RetireReadbacks` republishes when it retires something. Under C1-semaphore-poll copies retire up to ~100 us
  later, so the spans stay wider slightly longer: correct, just more lock fallbacks.
- `Spans` covers only valid, watched entries, the same filter as `RequiredTick`. Stale mode's `ReleaseOutside`
  (unwatch, CPU writes) narrows `RequiredTick` at once but does not republish, so the spans keep those pages until
  the next `Push` / `Retire` / `Cancel` publish: a superset, only more lock fallbacks. readback-first-wait's
  read-watched entries are still `watched`, so they are covered.
- Built with D4 / D5 / cache-line groups as "stage B bundle 2" for the Deck.

## Evidence

- Design: `obt_readbacks` 0.131 us a packet on the Deck. Expected 0.08-0.1 us a draw, ~0.1-0.14 ms a frame.
- Deck baseline 1 live pair `0 128` (C5 off): 73.7 vs 74.2 FPS, stage B 94.1% vs 94%. "+0.5 for C5 (noise
  level)". No visual issues reported. Not measured on the PC.
- `GOW3_FRAME_STATS` line "Readback pre-check per frame (C5 on|off): without the lock N, locked N". A high "locked"
  count means copies sit near the buffers and the spans need to be finer.
- A review on 2026-11-04 found no race in the seqlock or its callers. `readback-queue-test` covers `Spans`.

## History and pitfalls

- This is study-stage-b-per-draw.md's C5, not the "rescore C5" item.
- The win is a lock and a scan per call. It shows only while stage B is the limit and readbacks are always
