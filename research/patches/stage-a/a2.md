## What it does

`Rasterizer::PendingWriteOverlaps` runs for every small (up to `STREAM_THRESHOLD`) read-only buffer of every draw
that is not GPU-modified, mostly constant buffers (`CollectRingBindings`, its only caller). It tells whether the range overlaps a GPU write still queued for stage B.
If it does, the buffer goes to stage B instead of being copied into the constant ring. The min/max bounds rarely
answer, so it scanned `pending_writes`. A2 adds `Gow3StageA::PendingPages`: 4,096 bits of hashed 64 KiB pages
covering every pending range. When all of a query's pages are clear, it answers "no overlap" without the scan.

## Invariants

- Every byte of every `pending_writes` entry is on a set bit. All entries come from `NotePendingGpuWrite` (written
  buffers in `CollectRingBindings`; DMA_DATA, WRITE_DATA and fence labels in `liverpool.cpp`), which calls
  `pending_pages.Mark` on the new range before merging; a merge is the union of touching ranges, so it grows by at most the new range. The
  prune in `PendingWriteOverlaps` (every 64th call, after `erase_if`) runs `Clear()` and re-marks every entry left.
  A new path that adds or grows an entry without `Mark` lets the filter say "no overlap" for a range with a pending
  GPU write. Stage A then copies stale bytes into the ring: wrong per-draw constants, flicker.
- The filter runs **after** the prune, so the entry list and every answer are the scan's (exact, not heuristic).
- A range over 256 pages (16 MiB) sets `all`, so nothing is answered until the next rebuild. Queries of size 0 or
  over 16 pages are not filtered. A hash collision only causes a scan, the safe direction.
- `pending_pages` is maintained whatever the switch (`PendingFilterOn()` gates only the answer), so a live toggle is
  safe. Only stage A touches it, so there is no lock.

## Interactions

- lineage-constant-ring / ring-reuse: the answer decides between a ring copy (or reuse) on stage A and binding on
  stage B. A wrong "no overlap" breaks their data order.
- A1, A3: built and measured together (bit sum 7340032 = A1 + A2 + A3), all in the Deck preset.
- mausoleum-toggle-bisect: A2 was a suspect for the Hilltop Mausoleum lighting and was ruled out. The cause was
  pool-kept-pages.
- E1-deck-drains: the fence-label writes that `NotePendingFences` / WRITE_DATA put into `pending_fences` (E1's
  label check) are the same calls that feed `pending_writes` here; two lists, one call site each.
- Not rescore A2 (pool-small-copies). The IDs collide.

## Evidence

- Cuts redo (2026-11-07, draw preparation alive): A1 + A2 off (3145728) +2.0 points of a core at the 60 cap, +0.16 W CPU; uncapped no FPS change. Launch 15's A1-A3 +9.9 was mostly the dead draw preparation.
- Round 1, launch 7 (2026-11-05, Deck, launched by the user, live `0 0` vs `0 7340032`): A1-A3 78.1 vs 77.5 FPS, lows
  equal. A2's filter answers about 1,400 checks a frame, and the scans per frame drop from 1,225 to 0.4. At 1,200 MHz:
  +0.65 FPS, slightly better lows.
- Launch 15 (60 cap): A1-A3 off cost +9.9 points of a core of process CPU, with no watts. Uncapped, stage A is at 74.1%
  off vs 70.7% on.
- Caveat for every live A1-A3 number: the first flip of the A3 bit stopped draw preparation for the rest of the launch
  (A3's pitfall). Round 1 launch 7 and the 1,200 MHz pair (one launch: `data/deck-stagea-1.log`, `-1200.log`, 0 prepared
  from line 2646 on) and Launch 15
  after its first A1-A3 block ran with 0 prepared draws in both states.
- `pending-pages-test`: a model with and without the filter, 2 million steps x 3 seeds. Same answer at every query
  and the same list after every step; the filter answers about 49% of the queries.

## History and pitfalls

- Opt-in on Windows: not measured on the PC. A PC launch with the Deck switches and `GOW3_PENDING_FILTER=0` exists
  (Mausoleum repro, `data/t-mausoleum-pc-a2off.log`, untracked, no frame stats, not written up). The impl doc suggests A2 (exact, cross-platform) as a default
  candidate after a PC A/B.
- Expected -0.1 to -0.25 ms of stage A a frame (inferred). Its own share was not split from A1 / A3 in a live A/B.
